## Understanding the Dual Nature of Title Insurance

When closing on real estate in Texas, buyers are frequently confused by seeing two distinct title insurance line items on their Closing Disclosure:
1. **The Owner's Title Policy (Form T-1 / T-1R)**
2. **The Mortgagee / Lender's Title Policy (Form T-2)**

While both policies originate from the same title search and commitment, their legal protections, beneficiaries, and coverage durations are entirely separate.

---

## Comparison: Owner's Policy vs. Lender's Policy

| Comparison Feature | Owner's Title Policy (OTP) | Lender's Title Policy (MTP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who is Protected?** | **The Homebuyer & their heirs** | The mortgage lender & loan servicer |
| **Amount of Coverage** | Full purchase price of property | Declining loan balance only |
| **Duration of Protection** | As long as you or your heirs own the home | Ends when mortgage loan is paid off |
| **Is it Mandatory?** | Optional in contract (Customarily paid by Seller) | **Mandatory** for any institutional mortgage |
| **Protects Buyer's Down Payment?** | **Yes (100% of purchase equity)** | **No (Zero protection for buyer equity)** |
| **Legal Defense Costs?** | Underwriter pays all legal defense costs | Underwriter defends lender only |
| **Premium Frequency** | **One-time fee at closing (No renewals)** | One-time fee at closing |

## What Does an Owner's Title Policy Protect Against?

Even the most thorough title examination can be blindsided by hidden title defects that do not appear on the public county land register:

1. **Forged Deeds & Impersonation:** A prior deed in the 1990s was executed by a fraudster pretending to be the true heir.
2. **Undisclosed or Missing Heirs:** An estate transferred property without including an out-of-state child who later files a quiet title claim.
3. **Unreleased Deeds of Trust:** A mortgage from a defunct regional bank paid off decades ago that was never properly released of record.
4. **Defective Notary Acknowledgments:** Documents executed without legal capacity, under duress, or with forged notarial seals.
5. **Errors in Public County Records:** Mis-indexed deeds, overlapping metes and bounds boundaries, or tax liens misfiled under an incorrect parcel ID.

## Texas Simultaneous Issue Discount (TDI Rule R-5)

Under Texas Department of Insurance Basic Manual Rule R-5, when an Owner's Title Policy and a Lender's Title Policy are issued simultaneously at the same closing on the same property:
- The **Owner's Policy** is calculated at the standard promulgated rate based on the sales price.
- The **Lender's Policy** is discounted to a flat statutory fee of just **$100.00** (for loan amounts up to the purchase price).

This simultaneous issue rule saves homebuyers hundreds or thousands of dollars compared to buying standalone lender coverage.

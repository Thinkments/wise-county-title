## The Existing Survey Process in Texas Real Estate

A primary way real estate agents save their clients $600 to $1,500+ at closing is by utilizing the seller's **existing land title survey**. However, mishandling Paragraph 6C of the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract is one of the most common causes of contract disputes and closing delays.

---

## When Can Wise County Title Accept an Existing Survey?

For our title examiners and underwriters to accept an existing survey in lieu of ordering a new on-the-ground survey, the document must satisfy four legal criteria:

1. **Legibility and Surveyor Seal:** The survey must be a clear, legible copy showing the surveyor’s embossed or inked seal, Texas Registered Professional Land Surveyor (RPLS) number, and signature.
2. **Complete Boundary Depiction:** The survey must show all lot lines, building setback lines, easements of record, and physical improvements.
3. **Accompanying Notarized T-47 Affidavit:** The seller must execute and notarize the promulgated Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) **T-47 Residential Real Property Affidavit**.
4. **Lender Approval:** The buyer’s mortgage underwriting department must review and approve the prior survey.

---

## Paragraph 4 of the T-47: What Must Be Disclosed?

In Paragraph 4 of the T-47 affidavit, the seller swears under oath regarding changes made to the property since the date of the prior survey.

### Changes That MUST Be Written in Paragraph 4:
- In-ground or above-ground swimming pools added.
- Fences built, replaced, moved, or removed.
- Storage buildings, detached garages, workshops, or carports constructed.
- Patios, patio covers, decks, or room additions built.
- Utility, pipeline, or access easements granted across the tract.

### If No Changes Were Made:
The seller writes **"None since date of survey"**.
